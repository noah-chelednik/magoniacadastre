Rendlesham Forest, Suffolk, December 1980

Accounts of lights in and beside the forest between the twin air bases of RAF Woodbridge and RAF Bentwaters, on the nights of 25 to 28 December 1980.

The records below set the accounts of this place and time among the conditions recorded there. No verdict is computed for any account in this version: what is shown is context only, and no known-cause candidate is tested against any account.

What the records show

  • The computed sky at 26 December 1980, 01:00 UTC (night of 25/26 December): the Moon 39 degrees up in the south-east; Saturn 6 degrees up in the east; Jupiter 6 degrees up in the east. Saturn and Jupiter about 1 degree apart.
  • The computed sky at 27 December 1980, 01:00 UTC (night of 26/27 December): the Moon 30 degrees up in the south-east; Saturn 6 degrees up in the east; Jupiter 6 degrees up in the east. Saturn and Jupiter about 1 degree apart.
  • The Kp index is held for all 3 periods; its highest value, 3.33, falls in night of 25/26 December.
  • Station observations are held for all 3 periods, but their terms allow a link only, so no station values are given here.
1 more from the records
  • The reanalysis shows a temperature inversion at the nearest grid cell in 6 of 12 hours of night of 27/28 December, strongest 1.2 K; none in the other 2 periods it covers.

Sources

Records are copied only from sources whose licence verdict is open. Each source is marked by how it is used:

  • records included: the source's records are copied into the study data (its terms allow it).
  • derived results only: only figures derived from the source are given (times, places, counts, page references), never its text; for the three station archives with unknown terms (ISD, GHCN-Daily, IGRA), only from stations run by US federal agencies (the National Weather Service, the FAA and the US military), whose records are federal work.
  • linked only: the source is linked, not copied, and gives no values here: its terms are restricted or unknown, it could not be obtained, or (for those three archives) no station within reach is run by a US federal agency.

What could not be joined

  • No station observations, daily summaries or soundings are given: the station archives that hold them have unknown terms, and values are given only for stations whose held station history shows a US federal operator. Several stations within reach were US Air Force bases in 1980 (Bentwaters, Woodbridge, Lakenheath, Mildenhall), but their station records do not show the operator for that date, nor does the record of the sounding site (Hemsby, Norfolk), so all are links only (see above). The reanalysis is the only weather record here.
  • The Dst index, the solar wind and the magnetic observatory values are links only; Kp is given.
  • No open newspaper text exists for 1980 in the collections held (the open wire and newspaper collections end in 1977 and 1963).
  • No exact satellite passes (see above), no military logs.
  • No sounding is given, so the near-surface layer rests on the reanalysis alone, which is weaker than the soundings on shallow surface inversions (see Reading the records with care).
  • The computed sky has the Sun, the Moon, five planets and 16 bright stars. A full bright-star catalogue could not be obtained.
  • No source shows ducting above the lowest few hundred metres on any study night.
