Phoenix, Arizona, 13 March 1997

Accounts of lights over the city and the desert to the south during the evening.

The records below set the accounts of this place and time among the conditions recorded there. No verdict is computed for any account in this version: what is shown is context only, and no known-cause candidate is tested against any account.

Sources

Records are copied only from sources whose licence verdict is open. Each source is marked by how it is used:

  • records included: the source's records are copied into the study data (its terms allow it).
  • derived results only: only figures derived from the source are given (times, places, counts, page references), never its text; for the three station archives with unknown terms (ISD, GHCN-Daily, IGRA), only from stations run by US federal agencies (the National Weather Service, the FAA and the US military), whose records are federal work.
  • linked only: the source is linked, not copied, and gives no values here: its terms are restricted or unknown, it could not be obtained, or (for those three archives) no station within reach is run by a US federal agency.

What could not be joined

  • Hale-Bopp, the bright comet of that month, has no computed position: the sources of its orbit have unknown terms.
  • No open newspaper text exists for 1997 in the collections held; one newspaper archive of unknown terms is linked.
  • The Dst index, the solar wind and the magnetic observatory values are links only.
  • The upper-air archive's station list gives Phoenix Sky Harbor, 12 km from the reference point, as a sounding site from 1948 to 1998, but no Phoenix sounding is held here; the nearest sounding given is the nearest held one, at Tucson (171 km), so a closer sounding may exist in the archive.
  • The earthquake catalogue query held for 1997 asks for magnitude 2.5 and above, so smaller earthquakes near Phoenix are not counted; the 1952 and 1980 queries set no smallest magnitude.
  • The near-surface values come from the surface layer, which the soundings resolve only in part, and the nearest sounding given is 171 km away.
  • No open record of military flights or illumination flares for that evening is held; the Barry M. Goldwater Range and Luke Air Force Base, south-west and west of Phoenix, lie within reach.
  • The computed sky has the Sun, the Moon, five planets and 16 bright stars. A full bright-star catalogue could not be obtained.
  • No source shows ducting above the lowest few hundred metres on any study night.
