A place and a moment

Choose a place and a moment. This plate computes the sky there and gathers what the registry and its open records hold for that place and day. Every value names its source. No verdict is computed for any account in this version: what is shown is context only, and no known-cause candidate is tested against any account.

Worked examples

  • night of 19/20 July, 20 July 1952, 07:15 UTC; the study plate
  • night of 25/26 December, 26 December 1980, 01:00 UTC; the study plate
  • evening of 13 March (19:00 to 24:00 MST), 14 March 1997, 04:30 UTC; the study plate

The sky is computed for any moment; launches and stations are held widely; the geomagnetic index and the daily weather are held only for some dates in this edition, and a date outside them is stated as not yet held, never read as zero.

What this dossier holds in this edition
  • The sky: computed for any moment from the year 1000 to 2100 (less certain before 1875).
  • Launches: from 13 June 1942 to 16 July 2026.
  • Re-entries, counted per day: from 1 December 1957 to 17 July 2026.
  • Stations: from the published station lists of the observing networks.
  • The geomagnetic index Kp: 16 to 31 July 1952, 23 to 31 December 1980 and 10 to 17 March 1997.
  • Daily regional weather: all of 1940, all of 1941, January 1942, July 1952, December 1980, March 1997 and all of 2000.

More months are added with each update.

    How to read a dossier

    • Times are UTC. The sky is computed for the moment from published astronomical methods (Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 1998); against a modern ephemeris it agrees within 0.05 degree for the study dates. The sky disc is drawn as on the study plates: north at the top and east on the left, as seen looking up.
    • The terms used here are defined in the glossary.
    • Launches come from GCAT (74,621 launches dated to the day or month); re-entries are counts per day from the CelesTrak satellite catalogue (its terms allow figures, not copies); the geomagnetic index Kp is from GFZ Potsdam, held in this edition for 16 to 31 July 1952, 23 to 31 December 1980 and 10 to 17 March 1997. Kp is one index for the whole planet; this plate does not compute the geomagnetic latitude of a place, so it does not say how far toward the equator an aurora reached.
    • Weather: coarse regional daily values (cloud, precipitation, wind and temperature over land and coast, wave height and period at sea) on a 2 degree grid from the ERA5 reanalysis, held in this edition for all of 1940, all of 1941, January 1942, July 1952, December 1980, March 1997 and all of 2000. Each is an average over a square of 2 degrees of latitude and longitude (about 220 km north to south) and over a whole UTC day, not the local weather at a point or an hour. A month not yet held is shown as not yet held, never as zero. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information; ERA5 by Hersbach and others, Copernicus Climate Change Service, licence CC BY 4.0.
    • Stations are the published station lists of the observing networks, with the first and last year each network gives. A station listed as operating in a year may still have had gaps that year.
    • Sources are the registry rows whose stated places include the country, region or continent of the point, or the whole world, and whose stated years cover the year. A row that names a part of a country (a state, province or region) is listed only where the airfields near the point lie in that part; a few parts this page cannot place are listed for the whole country, and the row says which part.
    • The checklist names known-cause candidates present in the held records near the moment, each with its value and source. Context only: none is tested against any account, and an absence is stated only for the records held.
