Plate IVSurveyed 26 November 2026

Washington, D.C., July and August 1952

Radar returns that the air traffic controllers at Washington National Airport and Andrews Air Force Base did not match to known traffic, with accounts of lights, on several nights between 19 July and 16 August 1952.

The records below set the accounts of this place and time among the conditions recorded there. No verdict is computed for any account in this version: what is shown is context only, and no known-cause candidate is tested against any account.

What the records show

  • The computed sky at 20 July 1952, 07:15 UTC (night of 19/20 July): Jupiter 21 degrees up in the east.
  • The computed sky at 27 July 1952, 02:30 UTC (night of 26/27 July): Mars 20 degrees up in the south-west; Saturn 10 degrees up in the west.
  • The Kp index is held for 6 of the 12 periods; its highest value, 5.67, falls in night of 19/20 July.
  • The sounding nearest in time (WASHINGTON/NATIONAL, 5 km away) shows no temperature inversion for night of 19/20 July, night of 22/23 July, night of 28/29 July, afternoon of 29 July, night of 5/6 August, afternoon of 8 August, night of 9/10 August, night of 15/16 August; an inversion for night of 26/27 July (0.5 K, at the ground), night of 27/28 July (2 K, at the ground), night of 13/14 August (3.8 K, at the ground), evening of 14 August (2.2 K, at the ground).
4 more from the records
  • The reanalysis shows a temperature inversion at the nearest grid cell in 1 of 5 hours of night of 26/27 July, strongest 0.5 K; 4 of 6 hours of night of 27/28 July, strongest 3 K; none in the other 2 periods it covers.
  • The radar report entries held number 26: 20 radar returns and 6 assessments of radar propagation.
  • Press items held: 382 over 33 days, the most (40) on 30 July 1952.
  • No artificial satellite was in orbit: the first was launched on 4 October 1957.

Records are copied only from sources whose licence verdict is open. Each source is marked by how it is used:

  • records included: the source's records are copied into the study data (its terms allow it).

What could not be joined

  • Hourly station observations are held for 16 to 31 July only; there are none for the August nights. The Kp index is held for 16 to 31 July. The Dst index begins in 1957 and the solar wind record in 1963, so neither exists for 1952.
  • The hourly reanalysis at the reference point is held for 20 to 28 July (UTC), which covers the nights of 19/20, 22/23, 26/27 and 27/28 July and none of the later periods; the computed sky covers 19/20 and 26/27 July.
  • Earthquake catalogue entries are held for 16 to 31 July only; for the August periods no count is given.
  • The 20th Century Reanalysis (version 3), the first choice for 1952, could not be obtained; ERA5 was used instead.
  • The 1968 University of Colorado study is linked only; its readings of the soundings are not given here.
  • The radar sites' own recordings, their antenna heights, military flight logs, and the Project Blue Book case files are not held.
  • Accounts by private persons are not recorded here: private persons are left out by design.
  • The near-surface values come from the surface layer, which the soundings resolve only in part.
  • The computed sky has the Sun, the Moon, five planets and 16 bright stars. A full bright-star catalogue could not be obtained.
  • No source shows ducting above the lowest few hundred metres on any study night.

The study data page lists the gaps of all three studies.
