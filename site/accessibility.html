Accessibility

How the pages are made usable by everyone, and where they still fall short.

The standard

The site aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA, in light and dark colour schemes, on desktop and phone screens.

What is in place

  • Landmarks, a heading order and a link to skip to the text on every page.
  • Every control works from the keyboard, with a visible focus outline.
  • Text and interface colours meet the AA contrast ratios in both colour schemes.
  • Every chart and drawing has a table or a text of the same values beside it.
  • Motion is reduced when the system asks for it; nothing moves on its own, and nothing has a time limit.
  • The pages reflow to fit narrow screens and print without the navigation.

Known gaps

  • The dossier map is for pointing only; from the keyboard, a place is chosen from the list of places or typed as latitude and longitude.
  • The sky drawings are pictures; the positions they show are in the table beside each drawing, which is the version to use with a screen reader.
  • Some wide tables scroll sideways on a phone; each scroll box can be reached and scrolled from the keyboard.
  • Source titles are given in their own languages, and their language is not yet marked for screen readers.
  • The pages have not yet been read by people who rely on a screen reader every day; reports from them are especially welcome.

Reporting a problem

If something on the site does not work for you, write to noahchelednik@proton.me, naming the page and what went wrong. You will have an answer within five working days, and a fix where one can be made in the next release.
