# Method and scope

The Magonia Cadastre is a registry of sources: places where accounts of unusual experiences are kept, and the context
datasets that help place and date such accounts (weather, the sky, meteors, the magnetic field, earthquakes, aviation,
launches and more). Each row describes one source. The registry does not hold the sources' content; it says what each
source is, where it is, what period and places it covers, and on what terms it can be reached and used.

Beside the registry, the site offers a dossier for any place and moment, and three worked studies built from the same
parts. Everything shown is drawn from a named source or computed from published methods; nothing is written or guessed at
the moment of reading.

## What a row says

- **title**: the source's own name, in its own language, with an English gloss in square brackets when it is not English.
- **url**: the page that best describes the source.
- **tier**: A for a context dataset (weather, sky, geophysics and similar), B for a core source of accounts of unusual
  experiences (collections of accounts, case files, investigation files), C for the long tail (archives, journals, museums,
  report series and lists).
- **kind**: dataset, catalogue, archive, periodical, register or service.
- **topics**: one or more topic codes from a fixed list. They are search terms, written in the words searchers use, not
  this registry's descriptions of what a source holds.
- **time_start** and **time_end**: the years the source covers, when its pages state them; present for a source still
  adding records.
- **places**: country codes (ISO 3166-1), global, or a short region name, when the pages state them.
- **language**: the language of the source's own pages (ISO 639-1).
- **format** and **size**: as the source describes them. A size followed by a page name in brackets says which of the
  source's pages gives that figure; counted from a list means the figure counts the entries the source lists.
- **access_status**, **access_method** and **access_reason**: how a researcher reaches the source (below).
- **licence_verdict**, **licence_label** and **licence_evidence_url**: on what terms the source's data can be used, and the
  page that states them (below).
- **verified_on**: the date the row was last read against the source's own pages.

## Where the values come from

Every value in a row comes from the source's own pages: its landing page, its terms page and, where needed, its data pages.
A field those pages do not settle is left empty or unknown, never filled by guess. Some rows describe datasets listed in
large public catalogues; their values come from the catalogue's own record of the dataset, and the row links that record.
No row holds personal details of private persons.

## Dates

Three dates matter, and the site keeps them apart:

- the **snapshot date**, on every plate and in the manifest of the downloads: the day this release was built. Each release
  replaces the last; new releases follow every few days, with more rows and corrections;
- a row's **verified_on**: the day that row was last read against its source. Sources change after that day;
- the **years a source covers** (time_start and time_end), as its pages state them.

## Access: what a researcher faces

- **open**: usable without an account.
- **registration**: a free account is needed.
- **restricted**: access is paid, by request, or limited to some users.
- **ungettable**: nobody can get it now, for the reason given: offline (the source is gone), paywall, licence_refused, or
  not_collected.
- **unknown**: the source's pages did not say.

The access method lists how the source can be used: api (queries answered by a server), bulk (whole files to download), web
(pages to read or search) or manual (by visit, letter or request).

**not_collected** marks a small number of well-known databases of accounts that this registry lists, so a reader knows they
exist, but never collects from, by policy. Their rows carry the name and the address and nothing else.

## Licence: on what terms the source can be used

- **open**: research use is allowed without asking. Conditions may apply, such as attribution or no commercial use; the
  licence name and the terms page say which.
- **restricted**: the terms need permission or payment, forbid reuse, or reserve all rights over the data.
- **unknown**: no terms covering the data were found on the pages read. Unknown is an honest answer, not a gap waiting to be
  filled. Many sources simply do not say, and where a site states terms only for its own pages and not for records others
  made, the data stay unknown.

Where a row names a licence (CC BY 4.0, CC0, an Open Government Licence and the like), the label is shown, and the terms page
is linked as the licence evidence. When a source states one licence for its data and another for its descriptions, no label
is shown; the verdict and the terms page say it plainly. No terms text is reproduced here: follow the link and read the
source's own words before reuse.

Works made by United States federal agencies are in the public domain in the United States (17 U.S.C. 105). A row takes that
verdict only when a federal agency made the data itself; work by contractor staff inside a NASA centre, published through
NASA's own archive, counts as NASA's. Data that an agency hosts but a university, a cooperative institute, a foreign agency,
a volunteer network or another outside body made keeps its own terms, which are often unknown.

For data held by or about Indigenous communities, the CARE principles (collective benefit, authority to control,
responsibility, ethics) apply. A community's own protocol, when stated, governs use, and these rows give a country and a
region only, never the location of a sacred place.

## How far the rows can be trusted

The rows have a measured error rate, and it is stated here plainly. Rows chosen at random were read again against their
sources' pages, field by field. In the most recent of these checks, between 0.26 and 0.48 fields per checked row were found
wrong: about one wrong field in every two to four rows. The usual faults are a year, a format or a size read too loosely
from the source's pages, or a field left empty that the pages do settle.

Licence verdicts are held to a stricter standard. Among the rows published here, no licence verdict was found contradicted
by its source's terms; where a verdict could not be confirmed, it is published as unknown rather than open.

So a row is a reliable pointer, not a final word. Before relying on a value, follow the row's link: the source's own page is
the authority, and when a row and a source disagree, the source is right.

## The dossier

For any point on Earth and any moment, the dossier asks: what was in the sky there, and what do the registry and the open
records of the world hold for that place and day? It is put together in the reader's browser from the files of this site, and
shows the computed sky, launches, re-entries and the geomagnetic index, the weather where held, the stations operating that
year, the registry sources whose stated places and years cover the point, a checklist of known-cause candidates in the held
records, and the gaps.

The sky is computed from published astronomical methods (Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 1998); against a modern ephemeris it
agrees within 0.05 degree for the study dates. Where a source can be asked about a date and a place through the address of a
page, the dossier fills that address and offers it as a link to the source's own record. Only addresses tried and found to
open the right record are offered, and only for dates inside the span they were tried for. The link opens the source's site;
nothing from it is copied here.

The checklist is context only. No candidate is tested against any account, no verdict is computed, and the same checklist is
drawn for every place and moment. A line marked not found in the held records means only that the records held here, for the
span stated, do not show it.

## The three studies

Three dated, well-attested sets of accounts show what the registry is for: the Washington radar returns and accounts of
lights of July and August 1952, the Rendlesham Forest accounts of lights of December 1980, and the Phoenix accounts of lights
of 13 March 1997. For each, the registry's context sources are set beside the accounts' place and time: weather reanalysis,
upper-air soundings, surface weather observations, the positions of the Moon, planets and bright stars, space weather, and
satellites in orbit from open catalogues, and for 1952 the radar study's own entries and newspaper items of the time.

The study pages show records only from sources whose terms allow reuse. Sources with restricted or unknown terms appear as
links, never as copies; from them the studies take only the dates and hours of the periods, with one exception: three US
federal station archives (ISD, GHCN-Daily and IGRA) have unknown terms because they include observations from outside
suppliers, and the studies give derived values (ranges, daily extremes, inversions) only for the stations in them run by US
federal agencies (the National Weather Service, the FAA and the US military), whose records are federal work; every other
station in them is a link only. Each study page states what could not be joined and why. The joins are context only: they set
the accounts among the conditions of their time and place, test no known-cause candidate against them, and give no verdict.

## Using the registry

The registry's own rows (the descriptions, the fields and the licence verdicts) are released under CC0 1.0: use them for any
purpose without asking. Each source keeps its own terms; a row's licence verdict and evidence page say what they are. To cite
the registry, cite the snapshot by its date; the Downloads plate gives a citation in plain text, BibTeX and RIS.

## Limits

- The registry is not a list of accounts, and it holds none. A row says a source exists and how to reach it; it makes no claim
  about what the source's records show. A source being listed is not an endorsement of its claims.
- The registry is not complete, and does not claim to be. Sources that state no places or years are listed but not placed on
  the map of the dossier.
- The registry describes each source as it was on the row's verified_on date. Sites change, links break and terms are
  rewritten.
- Size, format and coverage are the source's own statements, not measurements made here.
